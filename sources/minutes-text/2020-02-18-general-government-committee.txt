6.1 Pickering Casino Update and Opening Transportation Management Plan
Presentation

John Grieve, Supervisor of Transportation, introduced Tim Arnott, Traffic Consultant, BA
Consulting Group Ltd., who delivered a presentation regarding the Transportation Master
Plan for the Pickering Casino. Mr. Arnott discussed the objectives of the plan, key
stakeholders, responsible authorities, opening event characteristics, casino activity, on-
site and off-site resources, street network and traffic flows, site access and circulation, on-
site and off-site parking strategy, transit strategy, emergency services strategy, signage,
traffic control and monitoring, police deployment, and communications strategy.

Committee Members asked questions and raised concerns regarding traffic impacts on
Bayly Street and Church Street, scheduled opening of the on-site hotel and entertainment
facilities, communication of the Church Street southbound road closure, satellite locations
for shuttle services, estimated counts of vehicles and visitors, signage and messaging,
impacts on surrounding areas, future road widenings and infrastructure improvements,
redeployment of Transportation Master Plan strategies after the opening period, staff and
parking resources available on opening day, traffic monitoring to be deployed, effects of
the approved minor variances on opening day activities and capacity, long-term impacts
on the surrounding area, and the Town’s communications strategy. Mr. Arnott and lan
Nielson, Manager, Security Operations, Pickering Casino Resort, responded to questions
from Committee Members and discussed traffic flow design, diversion of southbound
traffic from Church Street, scheduled opening of the hotel and entertainment facilities, use
of satellite locations for shuttles including Casino Ajax and the Ajax GO Station, vehicle

facility and number of gaming positions, maximum on-site parking capacity, shuttle and
transit routes to the site, future road widenings and infrastructure enhancements, ongoing
monitoring of traffic flows, and advertising for opening day.

Moved by: L. Bower

6.2 Energy Conservation and Demand Management Plan 2019 Update

Lindsay Sinclair, Capital Projects Coordinator, delivered a presentation regarding the
Energy Conservation and Demand Management Plan 2019 Update. Ms. Sinclair
discussed the purpose of the plan, regulatory requirements, results, 2018 greenhouse gas
(GHG) emission sources, 2011 vs. 2018 GHG emissions, implemented energy reduction
initiatives for facilities, outdoor lighting, and fleet activities, Ajax Community Centre
highlights, proposed measures for energy conservation, policy education and awareness,
and implementation of the plan.

Committee Members asked questions regarding potential further reductions to GHG
emissions from the Ajax Community Centre, costs and savings from LED streetlight
retrofits, and sustainability initiatives. Ms. Sinclair and Catherine Bridgeman, Director of
Operations and Environmental Services, responded to questions from Committee
Members.

Moved by: J. Dies
That Council approve the Energy Conservation and Demand Management Plan 2019

Update.
CARRIED

6.4 Community Safety Zone and Automated Speed Enforcement

John Grieve, Supervisor of Transportation, delivered a presentation regarding Community
Safety Zones (CSZ) and Automated Speed Enforcement (ASE). Mr. Grieve discussed the
background and history of CSZ initiatives at the Town, purpose and implementation of
CSZs, scoring criteria for potential CSZ locations, current and proposed locations, signage
requirements, ASE legislation and regulatory requirements, ASE deployment
considerations, best practices and lessons learned from other municipalities, financial
implications, and next steps.

Committee Members asked questions regarding engagement with Durham Regional
Police Service for enforcement of Community Safety Zones, scoring analysis, and
recommended locations. Mr. Grieve responded to questions from Committee Members.

Moved by: M. Crawford

1) That the report entitled “Community Safety Zones and Automated Speed
Enforcement” be received for information;

2) That the process to establish Community Safety Zones outlined in this report be
endorsed for use by Town staff in the future to evaluate potential zones in the Town
of Ajax;

3) That staff prepare the appropriate amendments to the Traffic By-Law for the
inclusion of the new Community Safety Zones for an upcoming Council Meeting;

4) That staff be directed to install Community Safety Zone signs in the newly
established zones;

5) That staff be directed to complete other associated tasks including, but not limited
to, sign installation and site preparation in each Community Safety Zone in
advance of the potential implementation of Automated Speed Enforcement in the
future;
General Government Committee, February 18, 2020

6) That staff be directed to monitor the implementation and lessons learned by other
Municipalities in Ontario who have deployed Automated Speed Enforcement; and

7) That staff report back to Council with an update on lessons learned from other
Municipalities who have deployed Automated Speed Enforcement and outline the
details of a potential Town of Ajax Automated Speed Enforcement in time for
consideration in the 2021 Capital Budget.

CARRIED
