Council / Management Community Action Plan — Planning Workshop

Strategic Plan Overview

Rachael Wraith, Acting Manager of Public and Strategic Affairs, provided an overview on
the strategic plan process and discussed the purpose of strategic planning, past examples
from previous terms of Council, and the difference between a strategic plan and the
Town’s current Community Action Plan.

Council members asked questions regarding outstanding action items from the previous
term’s plan, and public consultation on the plan. R. Wraith responded to questions from
members of Council.

Council Roundtable

Mayor Collier introduced and welcomed The Honourable Rod Philips, Minister of the
Environment, Conservation and Parks and MPP (Ajax), and Dev Ramkallie from the office
of The Honourable Mark Holland, MP (Ajax). Mayor Collier noted that the roundtable
presented an opportunity for all members to discuss issues heard during the 2018
municipal election, and to state their priorities for the coming term of office.

Among other matters, Council Members highlighted increasing traffic calming measures,
examining fees for recreational services, community safety and communication with
Durham Regional Police Service, economic development and job growth, on-street
parking and by-law enforcement, maintenance and beautification of parks, boulevards,
and the Town’s waterfront, fiscal responsibility, intensification and infill development,
enhancing partnerships with community organizations, improving regional transit,
addressing affordable housing, protecting the Town’s environmental assets, increasing
sources of non-tax revenue, expanding the number of Town events, and introducing a
tech hub incubator to Ajax.

Mayor Collier welcomed John Henry, Regional Chair, Region of Durham, to the meeting
and invited guests to comment on the priorities shared by Members of Council. The
Honourable Rod Philips, Minister of the Environment, Conservation and Parks and MPP
(Ajax), congratulated all winners of the 2018 municipal election and noted the strong
relationship Ajax has with the provincial government and that the province will remain a
partner with Ajax on key issues identified by Council. John Henry, Regional Chair, Region
of Durham, noted that all of Durham must work together to be heard by the provincial and
federal governments, and noted that transit remains a critical issue in Ajax and across
Durham. Chair Henry committed to working with Ajax to help achieve Council’s vision. Dev
Ramkallie spoke on behalf of The Honourable Mark Holland, MP (Ajax), and noted that
Mr. Holland looks forward to working with Ajax Council throughout the coming term of
office. Mayor Collier thanked all guests for their comments.

Fire Services

Dave Lang, Fire Chief, reviewed Town growth over the last decade, increased mandatory
health and safety requirements as a result of legislative changes, current staffing levels,
the Fire Underwriter’s Survey, and next steps in the development of an updated Fire
Master Plan.

Council members asked questions regarding staffing levels, mutual aid agreements with

insurance rates, and the possibility of a regionalized fire service. D. Lang responded to
questions from members of Council.

Planning & Development Services

Dave Meredith, Acting Director, Planning and Development Services, discussed
downtown redevelopment, economic growth and job creation, stormwater management,
the development approval process, external implications, the Durham Comprehensive
Review, potential development on the Carruthers Headwater, the Phosphorus Reduction
Action Plan (PRAP) study, Bill 66 — Restoring Ontario’s Competitiveness Act, and traffic

Members of Council asked questions regarding a comprehensive report on economic
development, extension of Hunt Street, traffic calming measures, foreign direct investment

Recreation, Culture & Community Development

Tracey Vaughan, Director, Recreation, Culture & Community Development, reviewed
pressures including aging infrastructure, facility upgrades, seniors programming, diversity

Culture Master Plan, need for an additional pool and/or therapeutic pool, a Town-operated
seniors centre, and community safety.

Members of Council asked questions regarding the Mill Street Fire Hall, development of a
community safety plan, competition with private fitness facilities, development of an age-
friendly strategy, and program affordability. T. Vaughan responded to questions from
members of Council.

Operations & Environmental Services

Catherine Bridgeman, Acting Director, Operations and Environmental Services, discussed
current staffing resources, increasing legislative requirements, new infrastructure,
implementation of an asset management plan, proposed levels of service, and capital
projects scheduled for 2019.

Members of Council asked questions regarding work on Church Street in 2019, potential
to implement paid parking lots as part of the Downtown redevelopment. C. Bridgeman
responded to questions from members of Council.

Finance & Budget

reserves, development charges, the asset management plan, and other fiscal challenges.

Members of Council asked questions regarding impacts to casino revenues from Durham
Live, costs associated with capital projects, and development of the asset management
plan. S. Strain responded to questions from members of Council.

2019-2022 Strategic Plan Process

Rachael Wraith, Acting Manager of Public and Strategic Affairs, reviewed key deliverables
and next steps in the strategic plan process and provided an overview of the engagement

Cannabis in Ajax

Rachael Matheson, Senior Communications Officer -— Government Relations, reviewed
the contents and recommendations of the written report and discussed recent decisions
to opt in or out by other area municipalities. R. Matheson reviewed the lottery process
conducted by the province and noted that only six locations would be available in the GTA
area, which includes Durham Region.

Council members discussed the results of the public consultation process and the
recommendations within the report.

Moved by: A. Khan
Seconded by: R. Tyler Morin

That staff be directed to inform the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario that the
Town “opts in” and will allow retail cannabis storefronts to operate within Ajax; and

That the Cannabis Policy Statement, included as ATT-1 to this report, be endorsed by
Council, and that staff be delegated authority to comment to the Alcohol and Gaming
Commission of Ontario, on behalf of Council, with respect to any store license application
that does not meet the public interest of the Town of Ajax based on the Town’s Cannabis
Policy Statement; and

That staff be directed to report back to Council prior to April 1, 2019 outlining
considerations and recommendations addressing additional bylaw restrictions on places
of use for cannabis consumption, building on the Smoke Free Ontario Act and Region of
Durham’s forthcoming updated Smoking Bylaw; and

That Council support requesting the province to include not only schools but also daycares
and other sensitive uses as appropriate within the 150 m buffer from cannabis retail
locations.

CARRIED

Bill 66 - Restoring Ontario's Competitiveness Act, 2018

Sean McCullough, Senior Planner, provided a presentation and reviewed the contents
and recommendations of the written report. S. McCullough discussed the Ontario Planning
Framework, the proposed Open for Business planning by-law and how it would operate,
and staff’s recommendations for comments to the province on the proposed legislation.

Members asked questions regarding where an ‘Open for Business’ by-law may be used,
existing planning tools to expedite development applications, optional public consultations
where such a by-law is considered, and the proposed provincial approval process.
Members discussed the merits of the proposed legislation in the context of existing
provincial regulations governing land use in protected areas.

Main Motion

Moved by: A. Khan
Seconded by: R. Tyler Morin

1. That Council supports, in principle, the use of Bill 66, Restoring Ontario’s
Competitiveness Act, 2018 to attract new major employment uses and investment in
Ajax with the provision that the Town passes Open for Business Planning By-laws
that respect its commitment to the Greenbelt, water quality, and Town land use and
environmental policies and plans.

2. That the following staff comments be submitted to the Ministry of Economic
Development, Job Creation and Trade regarding Schedule 10 (amendments to the
Planning Act) in Bill 66, Restoring Ontario’s Competitiveness Act, 2018 (EBR
Registry No. 013-4293):

a) Town staff recommend that development approved under an Open for
Business Planning By-law should conform to:
i. Provincial plans, policies and legislation; and
ii. | Municipal Official Plans.

b) Town staff recommend that municipalities continue to have the ability to
impose the site plan and architectural control conditions under Section 41 of
the Planning Act they deem appropriate for new employment uses approved
under an Open for Business Planning By-law.

c) Town staff recommend that public consultation be required prior to Council
passing an Open for Business Planning By-law to ensure the public is given
the opportunity to help identify opportunities and constraints on major
developments within their community.

d) Town staff recommend that the proposed Section 34.1(18) in Schedule 10 of
Bill 66 be amended to clarify that all sections of the Planning Act apply when
amendments or future business expansions to a development approval made
under an Open for Business Planning By-law are requested.

3. That the following staff comments be submitted to the Ministry of Municipal Affairs
and Housing regarding the proposed Open for Business Planning Tool (EBR
Registry Nos. 013-4125 and 013-4239):

a) Town staff do not support using an Open for Business Planning By-law to
convert employment lands within urban areas or introduce sensitive land-uses
into employment areas that may be incompatible. Town staff recommend that
the Province clarify, in the Planning Act, what secondary or ancillary uses may
be permitted in an Open for Business Planning By-law, based on the
underlying Official Plan land use designation; the scale of those secondary or
ancillary uses; and the criteria to be satisfied for permitting those secondary
or ancillary uses.

b) Town staff recommend that the Regulation under the Planning Act for the
Open for Business Planning Tool require new major employment uses to
provide a minimum job density target of 50 jobs per net hectare.

c) Town staff recommend that the proposed Section 34.1(18) in Schedule 10 of
Bill 66 be amended to clarify that all sections of the Planning Act apply when
amendments or future business expansions to a development approval made
under an Open for Business Planning By-law are requested.
