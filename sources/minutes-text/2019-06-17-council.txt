5.3 Letter of Support — Negative Changes to the Ontario Autism Program

Suzi Panovska-Guarrasi, Ajax Parent Advocate, introduced herself and discussed
changes to funding for needs-based therapy for children with autism in Ontario made by
the Provincial Government. Ms. Panovska-Guarrasi discussed her experience with
Applied Behaviour Analysis, cost to provide needs-based therapy for children with autism,
and negative impacts of the changes to the provincial funding model. Ms. Panovska-
Guarrasi asked that Council provide a letter of support on behalf of the community.

Elisha Chesler, owner of Sunshine Learning Centre, reviewed her background and
experience with providing Applied Behaviour Analysis in Ajax. Ms. Chesler explained how
needs-based therapy works and discussed challenges with demand for service,
implications of changes to the funding model, and loss of capacity to provide service as a
result of the funding changes.

Council Members asked questions regarding how the provincial funding model operates,
wait times to receive funding, effectiveness of needs-based therapies, waitlist for services,
out of pocket costs for parents, and resources available through the school system. Ms.
Panovska-Guarrasi reviewed wait times for funding under the new funding model. Ms.
Chesler discussed the effectiveness and benefits of needs-based therapies and reviewed
costs for families, wait times for service at her facility, and limited resources available
through the school system. Council Members thanked Ms. Panovska-Guarrasi and Ms.
Chesler for their delegation and noted the resolution on this matter later in the agenda.

9.1 William A. Parish Waterfront Trail — Mayor Collier & Regional Councillor
Crawford

Mayor Collier introduced the motion. Council Members discussed the legacy of former
mayor William A. Parish and his historic contributions to the Town of Ajax.

Moved by: S. Collier
Seconded by: M. Crawford

WHEREAS William A. Parish (1924-2018), a graduate of the University of Toronto and
teacher, moved to Ajax in 1952 with his young family to be head of the History Department
of the newly opened Pickering District High School in Pickering Village;

AND WHEREAS Mr. Parish became involved in Town affairs and was the Chairman of
the Ajax Citizens Association, which fought for and won the struggle in 1954 to make the
Improvement District of Ajax a “Town” with its own elected Council;

AND WHEREAS Mr. Parish served on the Town’s first Council in 1955, was elected again
in 1956 and 1957, and then became the Town’s third Mayor in 1958, serving six terms
until 1963;

AND WHEREAS during his tenure as Mayor, Mr. Parish enacted the Town's first Official
Plan, which designated a 400 foot wide strip of Ajax Waterfront as publicly owned open
space and greenbelt for the enjoyment of the entire community;

AND WHEREAS Mr. Parish also established the Town’s first publicly owned water filtration
plant to ensure a clean water supply for the community, and secure Ajax as an industrial
and commercial leader in the County of Ontario (now Region of Durham);

AND WHEREAS the protection of the Ajax Waterfront against private development was
an accomplishment Mr. Parish was particularly proud of, describing it as one of the most
far-sighted decisions the Councils of 1958 and 1959 made;

AND WHEREAS today, the Ajax Waterfront is a gem of unspoiled beauty which includes
over 240 acres of land with passive recreational space, community pavilions, parks and
a seven-kilometre Waterfront Trail, and is considered one of the most picturesque
waterfronts in southern Ontario;

AND WHEREAS the Ajax Waterfront Trail is now part of the Great Lakes Waterfront Trail
that stretches 3,000 kilometres from Gros Cap to South Glengarry and is also part of the
Trans-Canada Trail;

AND WHEREAS Mr. Parish received the Ajax Civic Award in 2005 — the highest honour
available to a Town of Ajax resident — for his dedication and continued support of the
community. He led and was involved with countless community organizations, including
Friends of the Ajax/Pickering Hospital, Pickering Ajax Citizens Together, and the John
Howard Society. He was also known as the Town’s unofficial historian, believing it was
important to honour and share the unique history of Ajax. For this, he posthumously
received the Town of Ajax Heritage Community Award in 2018;

AND WHEREAS Mr. Parish lived in Ajax for 66 years until his passing in 2018 at the age
of 94;

NOW THEREFORE IT BE RESOLVED THAT in honour of William A. Parish’s dedication
to the development, growth and prosperity of the Town of Ajax, the Ajax Waterfront Trail
be renamed to the William A. Parish Waterfront Trail;

AND THAT staff be directed to install two William A. Parish Waterfront Trail signs and
associated plaques and/or information panels at the east and west sections of the trail by
the end of summer 2019, coordinate an official unveiling event, and update Town of Ajax
promotional material and waterfront signage with stakeholders as necessary.

CARRIED
RECORDED VOTE:
In Favour: Mayor Collier, Councillor Bower, Regional Councillor Dies, Councillor
Khan, Regional Councillor Lee, Councillor Tyler Morin, Regional Councillor
Crawford

Opposed: None
