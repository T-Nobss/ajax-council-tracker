5.2 Traffic Calming Warrant Update

John Grieve, Supervisor of Transportation, delivered a presentation regarding the Town’s
traffic calming initiatives and warrant system. J. Grieve discussed what traffic calming is,
the history of traffic calming in Ajax, proposed evaluation process, Ravenscroft Road Pilot
Project, new traffic calming bollards, traffic calming projects, and project timelines.

Council Members asked questions regarding timelines and delays for future projects,
priority projects for completion, on-street parking impact of traffic bollards, transparency
of warrant scoring, future open houses, and completion of the Ravenscroft Road traffic

J. Grieve responded to questions from Council and discussed project delays due to
COVID-19, scoring of warrants, and details on forthcoming public open houses and
projects. He indicated that a list of projects could be shared on the Town’s website

G. Romanowski indicated that in coordination with Operations and Environmental
Services, speed boards will be deployed and appropriate line painting will be completed

9.1 Crossing at Haskell Avenue and Harwood Avenue North
Councillor Khan introduced the motion and reviewed its contents.

Moved by: A. Khan
Seconded by: S. Collier

WHEREAS residents have requested a pedestrian crossing at the intersection of Haskell
Avenue and Harwood Avenue North;

AND WHEREAS the intersection is frequently used by patrons of the retail establishments
on the east side of Harwood Avenue North;

AND WHEREAS there is no pedestrian crossing within 330 metres of the subject
intersection;

AND WHEREAS the full signalization of the intersection is not planned until the 2023-2027
timeframe;

AND WHEREAS an enhanced crossing would address pedestrian concerns and provide
for an interim solution;

NOW THEREFORE IT BE RESOLVED THAT:

Transportation staff evaluate the request for a crossing at the intersection of Haskell

Avenue and Harwood Avenue North, and report back to Council or the appropriate

Standing Committee by the end of June with considerations and potential options.
CARRIED

9.2 Ministerial Rezoning Order to Permit a new Long-Term Care (LTC) Facility in
the Town of Ajax, Region of Durham

Mayor Collier and Councillor Khan introduced the motion and reviewed its contents.
Council Members discussed the contents and wording of the motion, the location of the
subject lands and proposed employment impact of the rezoning. Council Members asked
Town of Ajax Council — May 19, 2020

questions regarding next steps with the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing, the
current owner of the property, Municipal Comprehensive Review Process timelines vs.
timelines for development with a rezoning order, vision for the current prestige
employment zoning, potential risks for rezoning, and allocation of provincial long-term-
care beds.

G. Romanowski responded to questions with clarification of the development approval
timelines and authorized uses and considerations used to factor the amount of
employment under prestige employment zoning.

Moved by: S. Collier
Seconded by: A. Khan

WHEREAS the Government of Ontario recognizes the urgent need to build 21% century
long-term care (LTC) facilities to ensure appropriate care (maintains the best quality of life
for residents as they age) is available for seniors when and where they need it; and

WHEREAS the Deputy Premier of Ontario and Minister of Health and Long-Term Care,
Christine Elliott, committed (in March 2019) to adding 15,000 long-term care beds into the
healthcare system in the next five years, and upgrading an additional 15,000 older long-
care beds to modern design standards; and

WHEREAS these additional long-term care spaces will ease pressure on hospital
infrastructure, easing “hallway healthcare” practices in hospitals and enabling nurses,
doctors and other healthcare professionals to provide better, more timely health care to
patients who are ill; and

WHEREAS Canadian-owned-and-operated Schlegel Villages has indicated its desire to
build a new 192-bed long-term care facility, employing 230 people, at 1401 Harwood Ave.
N., a project that aligns with Ontario’s vision of better care for our seniors including
trademark amenities, investment in people, transformational caring and integration with
the larger community that both residents and employees desire; and

WHEREAS the proposed development will addresses long waitlist times and lack of
specialized programming as identified by local stakeholders, including the LHIN and the
Durham Ontario Health Team, and further, it allows build-out of an integrated continuum
of care and Seniors Hub; and

WHEREAS the approval of this project will spark vital new investment (providing
infrastructure and construction opportunities needed to rebuild the economy after COVID-
19 shutdowns), including the proposed widening of Harwood North Avenue from
Woodcock Avenue to Taunton Road to accommodate growth in the area; and allow
Grandview Children’s Centre to complete their long awaited new 130,000 square-foot
headquarters for Durham Region children with special needs and families as part of this
process; and

WHEREAS the planning restrictions are impeding the development vision for these lands,
as the use is not currently permitted on the proposed site; and

WHEREAS consideration could be given to permit the development through the Durham
Regional Municipal Comprehensive Review (MCR) currently underway, but it will not be
completed until late 2022; and

WHEREAS the Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care has provided clear timelines for
long-term care beds to be operational, and there is a serious risk that bed licenses may
be lost; and

WHEREAS the Government of Ontario has supported similar projects, including
Tollendale Village 2 in Innisfil, and Durham Christian Homes Inc. in Clarington, by enacting
Ministerial Zoning Orders;

THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:

That Ajax Council fully supports Schlegel Villages in its effort to build a new long-term care
facility at 1401 Harwood Ave. N.; and
