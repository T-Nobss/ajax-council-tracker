Call to Order

Chair Tyler Morin called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m.

Disclosure of Pecuniary Interest

There were no disclosures of pecuniary interest.

Opening Comments

Sandra Serrao, Acting Director of Finance/Treasurer, provided a brief presentation and
overview of the Town’s 2020 Capital Budget and 2021-2024 Long Range Capital Forecast,
the contents of the attached report, and the recommendations contained therein. S. Serrao
discussed capital budget project categories, 2020 total capital budget allocation, funding
sources, general levy allocations, capital reserve allocations, reserve contributions by
source, spending cap recommendations, long-term and outstanding debt, federal gas tax
revenues, multi-year projects, and highlights of the 2020 capital budget.

Councillor Tyler Morin, Budget Chair, thanked staff for their work and reviewed the
contents of the proposed 2020 Capital Budget and 2021-2024 Long Range Capital
Forecast. Chair Tyler Morin noted that the 2020 Capital Budget and 2021-2024 Long
Range Capital Forecast reflects the strategic direction of Council and will effectively and
strategically manage assets of the Town.

Opening Public Comments/Questions
Chair Tyler Morin noted that public comments and questions would be invited both before

and after the committee’s deliberations on the capital budget document. He opened the
floor to public comments. There were no questions or comments from the public.

Review of Budget/Forecast

Chair Tyler Morin directed the committee through a section-by-section review of the capital
budget and report.

5.1 2020 Capital Budget and 2021-2024 Long Range Capital Forecast Report

Committee members asked questions regarding how assessment growth is forecast and
sought examples of projects under the ‘strategic initiatives’ classification. S. Serrao noted
that calculations are based on 0.75% assessment growth. Julie Mepham, Acting Manager
of Budgets and Accounting, provided examples of strategic initiatives which could include
projects such as master plans, environmental assessments, traffic calming, and capital
projects from the strategic plan.

5.2 2020 Capital Budget and 2021-2024 Long Range Capital Forecast Document

Questions and discussion focused on the following projects:
Studies and Other Initiatives

ENV001: Urban Forest Tree Canopy Technical Review

Committee Members asked questions about the timing of the project and inquired whether
it was necessary to pursue in 2020. Catherine Bridgeman, Director of Operations &
Environmental Services noted that the current plan is 10 years old and that updated data
would help inform future strategic initiatives.

The Committee acknowledged that a number of studies are scheduled to be completed in
the near future which will have unknown budget implications for implementation. Members
asked whether studies listed in the 2020 Capital Budget could be pushed back to 2021.
Shane Baker, Chief Administrative Officer, noted that studies are prioritized and vetted by
Senior Management and that Council may approve or defer such items.

Committee members requested information on the date a project was initiated in the
budget process to be included on future capital project detail sheets in order to help
determine the movement of projects through the budget forecast.

FINOO2: Financial Sustainability Plan Update

Committee Members expressed interest in updating the Financial Sustainability Plan
earlier in the term of Council.

Amendment
Moved by: S. Collier

That Project Number ‘FINO02 Financial Sustainability Plan Update’ be moved from the
year 2021 of the Long Range Capital Forecast into the year 2020 of the capital budget.
CARRIED

ECD002: Economic Development Strategy Update

Committee Members asked whether a five year refresh of the strategy was required when
the previous strategy was developed to cover ten years. Geoff Romanowski, Acting
Director of Planning and Development Services, indicated that the five year update will

provide more relevant economic information on which to base future directions and

CAO002: Corporate Branding Update

Committee Members asked questions regarding the need to update the corporate brand
and whether the project would include the Town’s website. S. Baker noted that the last
brand update occurred in 2005 and the community has changed over 15 years. He noted
the desire to have a consistent look and feel across the town and the need to meet
requirements of the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act.

ETP014: Automated Speed Enforcement

Committee members inquired whether community safety zones were a part of traffic
calming. G. Romanowski noted that in order to have automated speed enforcement work
effectively, community safety zones are required to put the infrastructure in place and sign
appropriately. He noted that the project will look at areas for proposed community safety
zones in more detail.

INF0O03: Acoustic Barriers & Entry Feature Condition Assessment

Committee members inquired about the age of existing acoustic barriers and whether
there is a provincially managed timeline for assessment. C. Bridgeman noted that the
initiative will review the age, structural condition and location of acoustic barriers and entry
features and comply with Ontario regulatory requirements to have all core assets
inventoried by 2021, and all others by 2023.

ETP010: Integrated Transportation Master Plan Update

Committee Members inquired why the timing of this project had been pushed back to 2024
in the 2021-2024 Long Range Capital Forecast. G. Romanowski noted that it had originally
been scheduled for 2021, however, a five-year review was recommended by the
Development Charges Study Consultant. The update to the 2019 Integrated
Transportation Master Plan (ITMP) will be used to inform the 2028 Development Charge
Background Study.

CCD005: AJAX 3D Letters Public Art Installation

Committee members asked for clarification on the cost of the project. Tracey Vaughan,
Director of Recreation, Culture & Community Development, indicated that the project cost
also included installation and the infrastructure required for the letters. She noted that the
letters are lit, and will be permanently installed and programmed at Pat Bayly Square.

CCD007: St. Francis Centre 10" Anniversary Public Art Installation

Committee members asked for clarification on the timing of the project. T. Vaughan noted
the importance of celebrating the anniversary of the community arts and culture centre,
and the opportunity for strategic streetscape branding to demonstrate the repurpose and
use of the facility to the public.

Technology

ITS002: Migration to Office 365

Committee members asked questions regarding the migration to a cloud-based model,
security, storage, and cost of implementation. Nicole Cooper, Director of Legislative &
Information Services / Town Clerk, indicated that the IT Strategic Plan consultants have
recommended the cloud-based Office 365 over Microsoft 2019 licensed ownership,
specifically for its storage and security benefits. Microsoft 2019 will be the last version
available to purchase, making transition to a cloud-based subscription necessary. She

noted that while the migration will be more expensive over time, it includes extra storage
access.

ITS007: Workstation Replacement 2020

Committee Members asked for clarification regarding replacement across the
organization. N. Cooper noted the shift towards purchasing mobile workstations, which
are more expensive than desktop computers. She reviewed the selection review process
to determine which job functions warrant replacement with a mobile workstation.

Town Buildings

BMT005: St. Francis Centre — Window Restoration & Roof Repl.

Committee Members asked questions regarding the need to move the project forward into
the capital budget from the year 2023, and the impact the project will have on access to
the facility. C. Bridgeman noted that damage is occurring on a regular basis. Restoration
of the stained glass windows was never completed and the projects are being combined
to minimize the impact to facility access, as both require the facility to be closed. She
noted that the windows will need to be removed, however, all work will be undertaken
onsite. The estimated duration of disruption is 6-8 weeks.

BMT033: Council Chambers AV & Interior Upgrades

Committee members asked questions regarding cost breakdown of the project, alternative
options for live streaming, and emergency replacement of audio-visual equipment, if
required. N. Cooper noted that the existing technology is 15 years old and failing frequently
and is a critical component of the project. She noted that the project supports elements of
the Council Strategic Plan. S. Serrao indicated that any emergency replacement would
come out of the capital contingency reserve. Project costs over $100,000 would require

Council approval. C. Bridgeman noted that if deferred, the cost of the project would likely
increase.

Amendment
Moved by: S. Collier

That Project Number ‘BMT033: Council Chambers AV & Interior Upgrades’ be moved from
the 2020 capital budget to the year 2021 of the Long Range Capital Forecast.

CARRIED

BMT030: Greenwood Pavilion — Flooring Replacement

Committee Members inquired about products that could be used to make interior finishes
more sustainable. C. Bridgeman noted that resilient flooring is industry best practice and
that replacement of the flooring is required due to safety hazards with the existing floor.
She indicated that there is a schedule for interior finishes projects to determine priority.

BMT026: — Town Hall - Roof Repl./Bldg. Envelope Repairs - Construction
Committee members inquired whether the project could be undertaken in part. C.
Bridgeman noted that the roof and cladding components are not recommended to be
separated. She indicated that by bundling projects, the level of disruption to the facility is
minimized.

BMT017: Town Hall - Interior Improvements

Committee members asked questions regarding the cost increase of the project and
whether the project could be completed more efficiently with closure of the facility. C.
Bridgeman explained how the project factored in work on evenings and weekends, as the
building will remain occupied while the project is phased for completion. She noted that
closure of the facility would require conversations with stakeholders but could be

BMT035: Main Library — Roof Repl./Bldg. Envelope Repairs — Constr.
Committee members inquired about the cost increase of the project. C. Bridgeman noted
that the 2019 consultant had provided updated costing through design.

Parking Lots

ENG002: Town Hall Campus Improvements - Design

Committee Members asked questions regarding the proposed timing with the forthcoming
Recreation & Parks Master Plan, project components, impact to the existing gazebo, and
safety concerns with the current parking lot design. C. Bridgeman noted the project is
necessary based on the condition of the parking lot, however noted it could be deferred
for a year. She indicated that it will also look to improve connectivity with a pathway,
lighting and gazebo upgrades.

That Project Number ‘ENGOO2: Town Hall Campus Improvements - Design’ be moved
from the 2020 capital budget to the year 2021 of the Long Range Capital Forecast.

ENG047: Bartlett Parking Lot Reconstruction

indicated that the parking lot has reached the end of its lifecycle and the new design will
incorporate permeable pavers.

ENV003: Parks Two Stream Waste/Recycling Containers — 2020

Committee Members asked for clarification on the two stream containers if the Town is
looking into third party waste collection. C. Bridgeman indicated that the project will
replace waste containers with two-stream recycling containers in the parks system. She
noted that green waste is not collected in the parks system due to animal access and
collection resource limitations.

Committee Members inquired why the project for Washroom Construction at Paradise
Park was not included in the 2020 Capital Budget. C. Bridgeman noted that staff will be
bringing forward a report to the Committee in January 2020 for review of alternate options.
S. Serrao noted that the capital project remains open.

ENG018: Rotary Park Boardwalk - Construction

Committee Members asked questions regarding the impact of erosion and flooding
hazards on the new bridge and funding source. C. Bridgeman noted that the Toronto and
Region Conservation Authority (TRCA) is undertaking slope studies and that the bridge
design has been approved by the TRCA. Stev Andis, Supervisor of Planning, Policy and
Research, noted that that Town continues to work with the TRCA regarding erosion. S.
Serrao indicated that this project is fully eligible for Gas Tax funding.

ENG019: St. Andrew's Improvements - Construction

Committee Members asked for clarification on the cost of the project, security
considerations for the St. Andrew’s Community Gardens, and whether the project should
wait until the completion of the Recreation & Parks Master Plan. C. Bridgeman reviewed
the components of the project, including proposed installation of a pickle ball court,
improved lighting, and outdoor amenities discussed with the seniors’ community. No
security cameras are proposed at this time. T. Vaughan discussed membership to the
Town’s seniors clubs, noting that outdoor amenities are accessible to anyone, and indoor
programs require membership. She noted that staff are aware that connection of the two
buildings could be a potential future project in the area.

Amendment
Moved by: S. Collier

That Project Number ‘ENGO19: St. Andrew's Improvements — Construction’ be moved
from the 2020 capital budget to the year 2021 of the Long Range Capital Forecast.
CARRIED

ENG080: AAC — Tennis Court (south) Upgrades — Construction

Road Networks

ETP015: Traffic Calming on Town Roads - 2020

Committee Members asked questions regarding the reduced cost, whether all projects
listed for 2019 had been completed, and the transparency of changes in the policy to
identify roads to implement traffic calming. G. Romanowski noted that all 2019 traffic
calming projects had been put on hold until the Town’s Traffic Calming Warrant process
is updated, to be completed in early 2020. Once updated, design for all 2019 and 2020
projects will be completed in 2020. He indicated that projects will no longer be listed
annually for completion in order to allow staff the opportunity to better allocate and
prioritize project needs with the available budget. John Grieve, Supervisor of
Transportation, noted that new traffic calming measures such as bollards are more cost-
effective, allowing for implementation of traffic calming on more roads. He indicated that
a public open house will be held to inform residents of the new warrant process and that
a report will come to the Committee for approval before changes are implemented.

ETP001: Westney/Millington Signal - Construction

Committee Members asked for clarification on the change in cost for the project. J. Grieve
noted that the Region of Durham is near completion of design and as a result, costs were
reduced.

ENGO039: Traffic Signals Rossland Road and Riverside Drive - Construction
Committee Members asked about the cost increase of the project. J. Grieve provided
rationale for the increase.

Committee Members inquired further about the status of Range Road and the project’s
movement through the capital budget and long range forecast. C. Bridgeman noted that
staff will look at costing once the Region has determined timing for watermain
replacement. J. Mepham indicated that the project was approved for the year 2018 and
will likely increase in cost when it comes forward.

ENG036: Rossland Road Widening — Church to Westney - Construction

Committee Members inquired why the project is proposed to be moved to years 2021-
2022, with costs increasing. Rick Chalmers, Supervisor of Infrastructure & Asset
Management provided a rationale, noting conflict with Regional projects.

Closing Public Comments/Questions

Chair Tyler Morin invited public comments and questions prior to the Committee’s final
consideration and approval of the 2020 capital budget and 2021-2024 Long Range Capital
Forecast.

Chris Baker, Ajax resident, asked if sidewalk repairs are included in the capital budget,
opportunities for cost-sharing with the Region on project numbers ‘ENGO45: Rossland
Road Detailed Design - Westney to Lake Ridge’ and ‘ENG046: Rossland Road Widening
- Church to Westney — Construction’, the planning rationale for project number ‘ETP001 —
Westney/Millington Signal - Construction’ and the election budget allocation in the General
Levy Funded Reserve. C. Bridgeman noted that sidewalk repair is captured in the
Operations & Environmental Services Operating Budget. Mayor Collier shared information
on the Regional Road Rationalization Study, noting that Rossland Road meets the
requirements to be a regional road and that there would not be cost sharing with those
projects. G. Romanowski indicated that with new planning practices being stricter on traffic
flow, the situation requiring retrofit will likely not occur again in the future. N. Cooper shared
information regarding components of the municipal election budget and noted that
candidate costs are funded by the candidate.

There were no further comments or questions from the public.
